📊 AdventureWorks | Business Performance Analysis (2011–2013)

📌 Project Overview

Developed an interactive Business Intelligence solution using Power BI to analyze AdventureWorks' business performance between 2011 and 2013.

The project focuses on understanding the key drivers of business performance through four analytical dimensions: Sales Performance, Product Categories & Subcategories, and Customer Analysis.

The analysis aims to examine revenue trends, evaluate profitability, understand product category contributions, and explore customer purchasing behavior.

Rather than relying solely on total sales, the project incorporates multiple analytical perspectives to provide a more comprehensive understanding of business performance and support data-driven decision-making.

🎯 Key Objectives

- 📈 Analyze sales performance and revenue trends across different time periods.
- 💰 Evaluate profitability and understand how efficiently sales generate profit.
- 🏷️ Investigate the contribution of product categories and subcategories to overall sales performance.
- 👥 Analyze customer purchasing behavior and their contribution to business performance.
- 🔍 Identify key business patterns through interactive visualizations and performance indicators.

1️⃣ Sales Performance Analysis

🎯 Business Objective

Evaluate sales performance between 2011 and 2013 by examining revenue generation, profitability, and profit margin across time periods and geographical markets.

The analysis is built around three complementary dimensions:

- Sales: Measures revenue generation and overall business activity.
- Profit: Measures the financial contribution generated after accounting for costs.
- Profit Margin: Measures the efficiency of converting sales revenue into profit.

These dimensions are analyzed together because high sales do not necessarily indicate strong profitability. A market may generate substantial revenue while contributing relatively little profit or operating with a comparatively low profit margin.

📊 Business Questions & Analytical Approach

1. How do sales evolve over time?

Visualization: Monthly Sales Trend

- Implemented a time-series line chart to monitor sales movements throughout the year.
- Incorporated a date hierarchy supporting drill-down analysis across Month, and Day.
- Enabled users to explore sales performance at different levels of temporal granularity.

Design Rationale:

A line chart was selected because it effectively communicates changes and trends over time. The date hierarchy provides flexibility to move from an overall monthly and daily analysis within the same visualization.

2. Does the business grow yearly?

Visualization: Monthly Profit vs. Previous Year

- Used a clustered column chart to compare monthly profit with Last Year's Profit (LY Profit).
- Enabled period-based comparisons to evaluate changes in profitability over time.
- Previous-year profit is unavailable for 2011 because the dataset does not include 2010.

Design Rationale:

The clustered column chart allows direct comparison between current and previous-year profit within corresponding monthly periods.

Profit was selected rather than sales for this comparison because revenue growth alone does not necessarily represent an improvement in the financial contribution generated by the business.

3. Which countries generate the highest sales, and how efficiently do they convert revenue into profit?

Visualization: Sales per Country

- Implemented a horizontal bar chart to compare total sales across geographical markets.
- Included Profit Margin in the chart's tooltips to provide additional profitability context.
- Applied conditional formatting to highlight countries with Profit Margins below the selected 35% reference threshold.

Design Rationale:

The horizontal bar chart facilitates comparisons across countries while keeping the primary focus on revenue generation.

Profit Margin is displayed through tooltips rather than as another visual measure to avoid overcrowding the chart.

Conditional formatting provides an immediate visual distinction between countries operating below and above the selected profitability threshold.

The 35% threshold was selected based on the business context of the analyzed product portfolio, which includes bicycles, spare parts, and clothing.

4. How is total profit distributed across countries?

Visualization: Profit per Country

- Implemented a donut chart to illustrate each country's contribution to overall profit.
- Used the visualization to complement the country-level sales comparison.

Design Rationale:

The sales chart identifies the largest revenue-generating markets, while the donut chart reveals how total profit is distributed across those markets.

Using both visualizations prevents the analysis from relying exclusively on revenue when evaluating geographical business performance.

📌 Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)

The dashboard incorporates nine KPIs covering financial performance, profitability, sales growth, and purchasing activity.

YoY Growth Calculation:

YoY Growth measures the percentage change in sales relative to the previous year.

YoY Growth = DIVIDE(
    [Total Sales] - [LY Total Sales],
    [LY Total Sales]

2️⃣ Product Category & Subcategory Analysis

🎯 Business Objective

Analyze how different product categories and subcategories contribute to overall business performance between 2011 and 2013.

The analysis focuses on evaluating product performance across three complementary financial dimensions:

- Sales: Measures revenue generated by each category and subcategory.
- Profit: Evaluates the financial contribution generated by individual product groups.
- Profit Margin: Measures how efficiently each product group converts sales revenue into profit.

The objective is to understand not only which product groups generate the highest sales, but also how their financial contributions and profitability efficiency differ.

📊 Business Questions & Analytical Approach

1. How do individual subcategories contribute to overall sales?

Visualization: Total Sales by Subcategory

- Implemented a column chart to compare total sales across subcategories within the selected product category.
- Enabled dynamic filtering through the Category Selector.

Design Rationale:

A column chart was selected to facilitate comparisons between subcategories and identify differences in revenue contribution.

2. How does each subcategory contribute to the selected category's total profit?

Visualization: Profit Contribution by Subcategory (Waterfall Chart)

- Implemented a Waterfall chart to illustrate the profit contribution of individual subcategories.
- Included Profit Margin in the chart's tooltip to provide additional profitability context.
- Displayed the overall category profit as the final total.

Design Rationale:

The Waterfall chart was selected to emphasize the contribution of each subcategory to the selected category's overall profit.

Including Profit Margin in the tooltip provides a second profitability perspective without introducing additional visual complexity.

This allows users to distinguish between the absolute profit generated by a subcategory and its profitability efficiency.

3. How does purchasing activity differ across subcategories?

Visualization: Total Orders by Subcategory

- Implemented a horizontal bar chart to compare the number of orders across subcategories.

Design Rationale:

A horizontal bar chart provides a clear comparison of order volumes across product groups and complements the financial analysis with a transaction-level perspective.

4. How does profitability evolve throughout the year?

Visualization: Monthly Profit Trend

- Implemented a time-series line chart to monitor monthly profit movements.
- Enabled users to examine changes in profitability throughout the selected reporting period.

Design Rationale:

A line chart was selected to emphasize the direction and continuity of profit movements over time, allowing users to identify fluctuations and changes in financial performance.

5. How do sales compare across years?

Visualization: Total Sales by Year

- Implemented a column chart to compare annual sales across 2011, 2012, and 2013.

Design Rationale:

A column chart enables direct comparison of annual sales values and provides a high-level view of revenue performance across the reporting period.

📌 Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)

The dashboard includes the following performance indicators:

KPI| Analytical Purpose
Total Sales| Measures revenue generated by the selected category.
Total Cost| Measures the associated cost of sales.
Total Profit| Measures the financial contribution generated.
Profit Margin| Evaluates profitability relative to sales.
Total Orders| Measures the number of distinct orders.
Total Quantity| Measures the total quantity sold.
Total Customers| Measures the number of distinct customers.
Average Order Value (AOV)| Measures average sales value per order.
Category Sales % of All Sales| Measures the selected category's contribution to total company sales.

Category Sales % of All Sales

This KPI measures the proportion of total company sales generated by the selected product category.

Calculation Logic:

Selected Category Sales divided by Total Sales across all categories.

DIVIDE(
    [Total Sales],
    CALCULATE(
        [Total Sales],
        REMOVEFILTERS(dim_products[category_name])
        )
    )

The denominator uses "REMOVEFILTERS" to remove the product category filter, ensuring that the calculation retains the overall sales context rather than being restricted to the selected category.

3️⃣ Customer Analysis

3️⃣ Customer Segmentation & Purchasing Behaviour Analysis

🎯 Business Objective

Analyze customer purchasing behaviour and financial contribution between 2011 and 2013 by examining customer segments across demographic, geographical, and sales-channel dimensions.

The analysis focuses on understanding how customer characteristics, including Gender, Marital Status, and Age Group, relate to sales generation, profitability, and purchasing activity.

Rather than treating customers as a single homogeneous group, the analysis explores differences between customer segments to provide a more detailed understanding of their contribution to overall business performance.

📊 Business Questions & Analytical Approach

1. How does profit differ across customer demographic segments?

Visualization: Profit Decomposition Tree

- Implemented a Decomposition Tree to analyze total profit through three hierarchical customer dimensions:
  - Gender
  - Marital Status
  - Age Group
- Enabled users to progressively break down total profit and explore the contribution of individual customer segments.

Design Rationale:

The Decomposition Tree was selected to support exploratory analysis by allowing users to investigate profit distribution across multiple demographic dimensions.

The hierarchical structure provides flexibility to move from an overall profitability measure to increasingly specific customer segments, helping reveal differences in financial contribution.

2. How much does each age group contribute to total sales?

Visualization: Sales Contribution by Age Group (Donut Chart)

- Implemented a donut chart to illustrate the percentage contribution of each age group to total sales.
- Included Total Sales in the tooltip to provide the actual sales value associated with each age group.
- Calculated each age group's sales contribution relative to total sales across all age groups, removing the Age Group filter from the denominator while retaining other applicable filter contexts.

Design Rationale:

A donut chart was selected to communicate the proportional contribution of each age group to overall sales.

Displaying percentages emphasizes the relative importance of each segment, while Total Sales in the tooltip provides access to the corresponding absolute revenue value without overcrowding the visualization.

3. Which sales channels generate the highest revenue?

Visualization: Total Sales by Channel (Treemap)

- Implemented a Treemap to compare sales performance across different sales channels.
- Included the following channels:
  - Online
  - Retail Store
  - Marketplace
  - Social Commerce
  - Wholesale

Design Rationale:

The Treemap was selected to represent the relative contribution of each sales channel to total revenue.

The size of each rectangle provides an intuitive comparison of sales volume, allowing users to quickly identify the channels generating the largest revenue contributions.

4. How does profit margin differ across gender and marital status?

Visualization: Profit Margin by Gender and Marital Status

- Implemented a horizontal bar chart to compare Profit Margin across customer segments.
- Examined Married and Single customers separately within each gender group.

Design Rationale:

The visualization focuses on profitability efficiency rather than absolute profit generation.

Comparing Profit Margin across gender and marital status provides an additional perspective on how profitability differs between demographic customer segments.

5. How is profit distributed across countries and age groups?

Visualization: Profit by Country and Age Group

- Implemented a clustered column chart to compare profit across geographical markets and customer age groups.
- Grouped profit values by country, with separate columns representing individual age groups.

Design Rationale:

A clustered column chart was selected to enable comparisons between age groups within each country.

Combining geographical and demographic dimensions provides a more detailed perspective on customer profitability across different markets.

6. How do customer-related sales evolve over time?

Visualization: Total Sales by Year

- Implemented a column chart to compare total sales across 2011, 2012, and 2013.

Design Rationale:

A column chart was selected to provide a straightforward comparison of annual sales performance and highlight changes in revenue across the reporting period.
